# The Glazing Perfectionist

Find the visual decision that could only have come from paying attention to this product. Carry it through the ordinary parts people actually use. Perfection is specific to the brief.

**Start with what is already good.** Use `lazy-architect` to choose a foundation when that choice is open. For interfaces, use the relevant guidance in `unslop-ui-design` for usefulness, accessibility, and rendered review. Look up these skills by name or beside this skill's resolved source directory. If they are unavailable, inspect the existing components, reuse the behavior that fits, and check that each visible element earns its place. An established identity is evidence to extend. A one-screen brief stays one screen.

**Find a point of view in the work.** Look at the actual material people handle, the decision they make repeatedly, or a distinction a newcomer might miss. Express the direction as a rule another designer could apply. “Editorial” could describe a thousand products. “Keep the speaker's exact words prominent and their source time aligned beside every quotation” can guide a transcript editor. When two directions seem equally plausible, try them on the same difficult piece of content and keep the one that makes the task clearer.

**Set type with the product's own words.** Put a real title, a typical control, and the hardest relevant string into the available faces at their working sizes. A price with decimals, a long bilingual name, and an ambiguous booking code ask different things of a font. Choose the families or system stacks, roles, weights, sizes, and line heights that make the hierarchy reproducible. Specify the fallback, numeral behavior, or script treatment where it affects the specimen. One existing family may do the whole job.

**Turn the idea into a small design system.** Decide which alignments, spacing, color roles, imagery, or interaction cues carry the idea. Give the signature a place where it appears, a meaning, and a place where it recedes. Apply it to a second relevant component or state within scope. Translate these decisions into existing tokens or component variants when implementing. For a visual refresh, express the idea through the content and actions the product already supports. Preserve familiar controls and semantic states. Reuse a reference's working principle while letting the product determine the composition.

**Make the call on the actual work.** For a build or mockup, inspect the rendered result on the target surface. For a critique, inspect what was supplied and distinguish a proposed change from one you observed. Cover the logo and imagine the accent color gone. What remains in the type, layout, or interaction that helps this product's user do something specific? Check that detail with inconvenient content or a constrained state. If the answer is weak, change the underlying decision and recheck the affected surface. Finish when the signature holds, the core task is clear, and the brief is met. Give a concrete verdict and point to the detail behind it. Say when visual verification was unavailable.

For precedents on choosing a type specimen or carrying a signature into a second context, see [design studies](references/design-studies.md).
